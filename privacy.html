Privacy Policy

Effective 11 September 2026

OverviewInformationHow we use itProvidersRetentionYour choicesContact

This policy describes the current CrateSeek beta. Apple sign-in is optional while account setup is completed. CrateSeek does not sell personal information and does not ask for App Tracking Transparency permission. Where offered, optional rewarded video ads on the Free plan come from Google AdMob and are described in section 4.

1. Overview

CrateSeek is a music-discovery app for producers. Most preferences and library activity stay on your device. We process a small amount of pseudonymous information to operate the catalog, understand whether core features work and receive feature requests.

2. Information we process

Stored on your device: onboarding status, filter and playback preferences, saved tracks, listening history, IDs of tracks already shown, an anonymous installation ID, a short queue of analytics events waiting to be sent, your progress through the current block of free discoveries, and an encrypted Supabase session when you sign in. When ads are offered, Google’s consent tool also stores your ad privacy choices on the device.

Sent to CrateSeek: an email address you submit to the website waitlist; your Apple account identifier, relay or shared email address, and name when Apple provides it and you choose to sign in; Founder access status; the names of Crates you create and which saved recording IDs you place in them; random installation and session IDs; limited product events; relevant recording or playback-source IDs; timestamps; app and platform details; sanitized crash frames; and feature-request text you deliberately submit. Crash events do not include the error message, source-code context, screen contents, URL query parameters, local user paths or user-entered text. We do not intentionally collect precise location, contacts, microphone recordings or music files.

Technical request data: hosting and infrastructure providers may receive standard network information such as IP address, user agent and request time. CrateSeek uses one-way hashed, short-lived request buckets for abuse prevention and does not store raw IP addresses in its application database.

3. How we use information

  • Provide playable catalog results and remember tracks already shown.
  • Link Founder access to your account.
  • Maintain saved tracks, history, Crates and your preferences, and sync Crates across devices signed in to the same account.
  • Notify waitlist members when CrateSeek becomes available.
  • Measure whether onboarding, playback, discovery and saving work reliably.
  • Prevent spam, bots and excessive requests.
  • Review feature ideas you choose to submit.
  • Offer optional rewarded video ads that unlock more discoveries on the Free plan.
  • Protect CrateSeek and comply with legal obligations.

For users in the European Economic Area, processing necessary to deliver requested features is based on performance of the service or steps you request before using it; limited reliability analytics and abuse prevention are based on our legitimate interests in operating and securing CrateSeek. Where the law requires it, ad storage, measurement and personalization rely on the consent you give in Google’s consent message.

4. Providers and external playback

CrateSeek uses Apple for optional account authentication; Supabase for authentication, the waitlist, catalog data and server-side functions; PostHog Cloud for privacy-minimal product analytics and error tracking when configured; GitHub Pages for this website; and YouTube’s permitted embedded player for playable previews. PostHog receives pseudonymous events and sanitized JavaScript crash frames, and is configured without person profiles, session replay, broad interaction autocapture, console capture or GeoIP enrichment. CrateSeek does not host, extract or offer downloads of third-party music. When a YouTube player or external link is opened, Google/YouTube may receive device and network information under its own terms and privacy policy. Discogs and MusicBrainz provide catalog metadata; CrateSeek’s trusted catalog tools access them server-side or on the project owner’s machine.

Optional rewarded video ads. When ads are switched on, Free users can choose to watch a short video from Google AdMob to unlock more discoveries. A video only plays after you tap to unlock; Pro and Founder members never see ads. To deliver and measure ads, prevent fraud and, where you allow it, personalize them, Google may process device and network information such as your IP address (which Google uses to estimate approximate location), device and app identifiers other than Apple’s advertising identifier, and ad interaction and diagnostic data. Because CrateSeek does not ask for App Tracking Transparency permission, Apple’s advertising identifier (IDFA) is not available to Google through CrateSeek. In the EEA, the UK, Switzerland and other places where consent is required, Google’s consent message asks for your choices before any ad is requested, and you can change them at any time under Profile → Privacy Choices. If you decline, CrateSeek keeps working and may show limited, non-personalized ads or none. CrateSeek itself only records pseudonymous events about unlock attempts (for example, whether a video was completed), linked to the random installation ID, and never receives your ad choices or advertising identifiers. Google’s processing is governed by the Google Privacy Policy and how Google uses information from apps that use its services.

RevenueCat SDKs are not present in the current beta; this policy and the App Store privacy disclosures must be reviewed before RevenueCat is enabled. We do not authorize current providers to use CrateSeek’s private waitlist or feature-request inbox for their own advertising. Their independent processing is governed by their own policies.

5. Retention and security

Device data remains until you clear the app’s data or remove the app. Account, Founder and Crate records remain while your account exists and are permanently deleted when you delete your account. Waitlist emails remain until launch communication is complete or you ask us to remove them. Feature requests and pseudonymous analytics are retained only while reasonably useful for product planning, reliability, security and legal needs, after which they are deleted or aggregated. Security controls include encrypted transport, least-privilege database access, row-level security, input limits, rate limits and restricted server-side keys.

No system can guarantee absolute security. Please do not place confidential, legally privileged or highly sensitive information in a feature request.

6. Your choices and rights

You can continue without Apple sign-in during the beta and remove local CrateSeek data by clearing app storage or uninstalling the app. If you signed in, you can permanently delete your account at any time in the app under Profile → Delete account; this removes your account, Founder access, Crates and the data CrateSeek stores on that device, and revokes CrateSeek’s Sign in with Apple access. Deleting your account does not cancel an App Store subscription; manage that in your App Store settings. Depending on where you live, you may have rights to request access, correction, deletion, restriction or objection concerning information associated with your email, account or installation ID. Fully anonymous records may not be linkable to you.

7. Children

CrateSeek is not directed to children under 13. If you believe a child has submitted personal information, contact us so it can be reviewed and removed.

8. Changes

We may update this policy when the app, providers or legal requirements change. The effective date at the top will be updated, and material changes will be communicated through the app or website where appropriate.

9. Contact

For privacy questions, use the contact method listed on the CrateSeek project owner’s GitHub profile. Do not include private information in a public GitHub issue.
